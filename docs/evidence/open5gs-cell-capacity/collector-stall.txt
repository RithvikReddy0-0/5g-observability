Mechanism: the per-run tally kept every delay sample in Python lists from one /run/start to the
next; the previous KPI run had ended ~10.5 h earlier, so ~19 million samples (+ as many jitter
values; the container used 552 MiB at 00:00, 4 h in). /run/start replaced the tally while holding
the lock the receive loop needs, the receive loop stopped reading, the buffer filled in ~1 s and the
kernel dropped the rest. Freeing 10 million warm list samples under a lock stalled a competing thread
for 0.15 s in a benchmark on this host, so the ~9 s here implies the 10-hour-old memory was cold
(paged out by the WSL2 VM or Windows); that last step is inferred, not measured. The fix removes the
mechanism either way: samples are flat arrays freed in one call, capped at 4 million per run, and
the old tally is released outside the lock; the receive buffer is forced to 8 MiB; and the collector
now reports kpi_receiver_drops_total and, per run, receiver_drops, which run_kpis.py records and
warns about, so instrument loss is never again counted as network loss without a flag.

After the fix (round 5, 20261109-074847Z and -075428Z): receiver_drops 0 and 0; dn-urllc 13 MiB.
